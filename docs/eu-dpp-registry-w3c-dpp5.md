answer_summary: 'The European Commission’s October 2026 EU DPP Registry package provides common product-registration
  interfaces, semantic resources and access guidance built on open technologies such as JSON-LD, RDF, SKOS and SHACL.
  Spherity’s DPP4.0/DPP5.0 model describes proposed steps beyond semantic interoperability: issuer-bound evidence,
  current authority, governed access and connected evidence graphs. The proposed DPP + European Business Wallet
  convergence could supply organizational identity and mandates, but still requires accepted trust profiles, sector
  governance and local enforcement.'
abstract: The European Commission released its Digital Product Passport (DPP) Registry technical package on 30 October
  2026, combining registration and authentication guidance with semantic assets for product data. This paper examines
  the division between a common EU Registry and distributed product-passport content, and explains how Resource
  Description Framework (RDF), JSON-LD, SKOS, SHACL and related standards contribute shared meaning, data validation
  and machine-readable discovery. It then introduces DPP4.0 and DPP5.0 as Spherity-defined maturity stages rather
  than adopted EU categories. DPP4.0 adds verifiable issuer-bound claims; DPP5.0 connects claims, sources, status
  and dependencies in access-controlled evidence graphs. A fictional product and robot-evidence example illustrates
  why provenance and claim status matter for industrial AI. The paper assesses current Registry functions against
  this model and proposes convergence with European Business Wallets, W3C Verifiable Credentials and machine-readable
  policy to support legal-person identity, representation, bounded mandates and controlled use. The proposed stack
  is an architectural recommendation. Its legal recognition, trust profiles, cross-company interoperability and
  operational assurance remain to be specified and tested.
key_takeaways:
- The Registry registers identifiers and related data; it does not need to centralize every product-passport document
  or evidence file.
- The Commission package introduces machine-readable semantic assets and APIs; validation against SHACL does not
  establish that a product claim is true or the issuer authorized.
- DPP4.0 / DPP 4.0 and DPP5.0 / DPP 5.0 are Spherity research stages, not EU regulatory or W3C maturity classifications.
- Verifiable Credentials can bind claims to issuers; verified legal-person identity, recognized roles, current status
  and policy remain separate conditions.
- A future European Business Wallet can help establish corporate identity and representation across borders, but
  the regulation is still a legislative proposal.
- Evidence graphs can make source changes and dependent claims traceable; revocation, interpretation and downstream
  action need explicit receiver-side rules.

questions_answered:
- question: What does the EU DPP Registry actually store?
  answer: The Registry stores product-passport identifiers and required registration information under applicable
    EU rules. The product-passport payload can remain at the responsible economic operator or another permitted
    host. Registration alone does not prove every claim is true.
- question: What is new in the 30 October 2026 DPP technical package?
  answer: The package includes the Semantic Data Guide, machine-readable semantic artifacts, API Integration Guide,
    OpenAPI specifications, Service Account Integration Guide, and guidance for self-hosted authentication and authorization.
- question: Which W3C technologies underpin the DPP semantic model?
  answer: RDF provides graph semantics; JSON-LD binds JSON properties to global meanings; SKOS defines controlled
    concepts; SHACL validates graph constraints; SPARQL queries graphs; OWL supports ontology semantics; and DCAT
    supports catalogues. XSD supplies relevant datatypes and XML definitions.
- question: What do DPP4.0 and DPP5.0 mean?
  answer: DPP4.0 (DPP 4.0) and DPP5.0 (DPP 5.0) are Spherity research labels. DPP4.0 adds attributable, verifiable
    claims; DPP5.0 connects current product evidence, provenance, authority and purpose-specific policy across companies.
    They are not EU maturity classes.
- question: Does SHACL verification prove a product claim is true?
  answer: No. SHACL tests conformance to declared data shapes and constraints. Factual reliability requires competent
    sources, evidence checks and potentially independent assessment; authorized use requires a separate policy decision.
- question: Does the DPP Registry require W3C Verifiable Credentials?
  answer: The October 2026 technical package does not establish W3C Verifiable Credentials as a general requirement
    for all product claims. Signed credentials can be a complementary evidence format under future or sector-specific
    profiles.
- question: How can European Business Wallets support DPP transactions?
  answer: A future European Business Wallet may supply reusable business identity, representation credentials and
    bounded mandates. Verifiable evidence, credential status and relying-party authorization can then be linked
    to the DPP, subject to final law, accepted trust frameworks and local enforcement.
- question: What remains to be tested for DPP5.0?
  answer: Interoperable issuance and verification profiles, authority recognition, policy decisions, evidence status
    and revocation, cross-company graph links, provenance completeness, latency, failure handling and auditability
    need implementation tests.

<h2 id="what-the-registry-does">What the EU DPP Registry does—and does not do</h2>

The European Commission published a major technical package for the **Digital Product Passport (DPP) Registry on 30 October 2026**. The package includes the Semantic Data Guide, semantic artifacts, API specifications, service-account integration and self-hosted authentication guidance. It makes the semantic and technical shape of the Registry much more concrete.<a class="citation-ref" href="#ref-1">[1]</a>

The Registry provides a common EU registration and indexing point for product identifiers and related metadata, while the accompanying semantic resources establish shared definitions and validation rules.  The Digital Product Passport itself can be hosted by the responsible economic operator. A successful registration or schema check must not be read as independent proof that every underlying product claim is true. This visual paper addresses three questions:

1. **What semantic infrastructure has the EU actually implemented?**
2. **Which trust and policy functions remain outside the current Registry package?**
3. **How could Digital Product Passports and European Business Wallets converge into DPP5.0: access-controlled, verifiable evidence graphs for products, AI and physical AI?**

The analysis separates EU law and technical implementation from Spherity's proposed maturity model. **DPP1.0–DPP5.0 is a Spherity framing. DPP4.0 / DPP 4.0 and DPP5.0 / DPP 5.0 are not official EU or W3C terms.** The DPP + European Business Wallet (EBW) convergence described below is a target architecture and standards recommendation.

The W3C standards have different jobs. RDF/JSON-LD express statements and their meaning; SKOS defines controlled values; SHACL checks whether data meets stated shapes; SPARQL queries graphs; OWL supports machine-interpretable ontology semantics. The EU DCAT-AP profile is based on the W3C DCAT vocabulary. These technologies do **not**, without a separate profile, make statements cryptographically verifiable or grant permission to perform an action.

The important implementation distinction is **data format versus shared meaning**. JSON remains the mandatory baseline registration format in the current implementation. JSON-LD and RDF preserve graph semantics directly. For DPPs that will later participate in signed evidence graphs, using the EU JSON-LD contexts can reduce mapping work between product data and a separately specified verifiable-claim profile. A JSON-LD representation does not supply a proof or trusted issuer by itself.

<h2 id="worked-example">Worked example: a battery passport and a withdrawn test report</h2>

Consider an illustrative industrial battery. The manufacturer registers the relevant identifier and required information with the EU DPP Registry. Its DPP points to a materials declaration from a supplier and a laboratory report supporting a claimed property. A recycler later requests permitted information about the battery.

1. **Registration and meaning (current infrastructure):** the product identity and applicable fields use the Registry model, and the documents can remain at their source systems.
2. **Evidence issuance (proposed DPP4.0 layer):** the supplier and laboratory issue signed statements. A verifier checks their integrity and the recognized authority of each issuer separately.
3. **Evidence linking (proposed DPP5.0 layer):** the product claim links to the test report, relevant configuration, validation method and current status. The recycler receives a view according to its role and purpose.
4. **Correction and withdrawal:** if the laboratory withdraws the report, systems using that report can identify dependent product claims and reassess them. A HOLD or other response requires explicit rules and receiver-side enforcement; it does not happen automatically because a graph exists.

This is a **conceptual example**, not a reported EU Registry integration or tested battery deployment. It illustrates the division between semantics, source evidence, issuer authority, status and purpose-bound access.

<h2 id="ai-and-evidence-graphs">From semantic product data to AI-ready evidence</h2>

AI systems need more than readable fields. They need to know **what a statement means, who asserted it, which source and version support it, whether it remains current, and which downstream decisions depend on it**. Physical AI adds a further requirement: a withdrawn test, calibration or conformity claim may have to propagate to affected machine configurations or release decisions.

### How DPP + EBW convergence divides responsibilities

The convergence model does not turn the Business Wallet into a product database. It separates responsibilities:

- **DPP / DPP Registry:** product identity, product semantics, registration, discovery, DPP validation and product-linked evidence.
- **European Business Wallet:** legal-person identity, representation, trusted organizational documents, signatures/seals and bounded delegation where the final legislation and profiles support them.
- **Verifiable Credentials and current status:** portable, issuer-bound evidence about identity, roles, permits, conformity, testing or other claims under governed profiles.
- **Policy decision and enforcement:** the relying party's decision on whether a particular actor may obtain or change particular evidence for a stated purpose and context.
- **Evidence graph:** the machine-traversable connection between product facts and the authoritative evidence that supports them.

Together, these components **could address** the gap between **“what is known about the product?”** and **“who is authorised to assert, access or act on that evidence now?”** The gap is not automatically closed by connecting a wallet: recognized issuer roles, sector rules, revocation/status, interoperability profiles, policy decision points and enforcement still need to be implemented and tested.

The European Business Wallet is still legislation in progress. The Commission proposed COM(2025) 838 in November 2025. The Council adopted its negotiating position on 9 June 2026. The European Parliament's responsible committee voted on 10 September 2026, and plenary confirmed the mandate to enter interinstitutional negotiations on 7 October 2026. As of the evidence cut-off, the procedure remains at first reading.<a href="#ref-15">[15]</a> <a href="#ref-16">[16]</a> <a href="#ref-17">[17]</a>

The stack should remain modular. The European Business Wallet is a proposed EU legal/business instrument, not a W3C specification. W3C credentials do not establish legal authority by themselves; an EBW does not prove a product claim is true; and ODRL does not determine policy governance. The relying party must combine trusted issuers, current status, sector rules and local authorization.

<h2 id="recommendations">Engineering recommendations</h2>

**Implement what is required now; profile and test the proposed trust layer separately.** The current Commission semantic and API documentation should be the technical baseline. Future wallet, credential and policy integration should be treated as a controlled interoperability programme, not as an already-required feature of the Registry.

1. **Industry: implement the required registration format first; use JSON-LD for richer operational evidence where practical.** The Commission’s contexts map JSON fields to defined semantics. Adopt them where suitable, and verify that any transformation preserves the required registry payload and validation rules.
2. **DPP and EBW standards work: align product evidence with open credential profiles.** Legal-person identity, representation, role, mandate and product evidence should interoperate without proprietary translation layers.
3. **Policy layer: make protected access machine-readable.** Define purpose, role, resource, action, context, validity and status as explicit policy inputs; evaluate them on every material request.
4. **Keep current EU capabilities and target architecture separate.** Label DPP5.0, evidence-graph profiles and DPP + EBW convergence as research proposals until adopted profiles exist.
5. **Test the whole verification path.** Conformance work should cover semantic versions, claim signatures, issuer recognition, credential status, delegation, policy decisions, cross-company linking, withdrawal and failure cases.
6. **Preserve source authority.** The Registry, a wallet or an evidence graph should not turn derived or copied data into a new authoritative source without explicit issuer governance.
